# Imputation methods and EDA charts for skewed claims

Scope note for the report writer: these notes compare imputation methods and name the charts a student notebook needs for right-skewed, zero-inflated, capped insurance-claim amounts with categorical codes, rare levels, and informative missingness. They do not assign a method to any NFIP column and they do not restate the OpenFEMA field dictionary. Coverage limits and coordinate rounding are included only because they change the mean-versus-median choice, the raw-versus-log choice, and the map.

Python versions checked against public docs in this pass: scikit-learn imputation pages labeled 1.9.1 (one user-guide URL was labeled 1.9.0), seaborn 0.13.2, missingno README plus PyPI listing 0.5.2, missForest CRAN 1.6.1 (packaged 2025-10-22). The student's installed versions were not checked.

## For each method, what is it, when does it help, what does it distort, and what should the notebook show?

### Takeaway

Univariate fills (mean, median, mode, constant) are the only methods that are routinely cheap on hundreds of thousands of rows. They keep every row, but they glue a spike into the distribution, shrink variance, and weaken or invent relationships. Donor methods (predictive mean matching, hot deck) and stochastic regression are the ones that can keep claim amounts inside the range of observed dollars. Multivariate methods (iterative imputation, missForest, k-nearest neighbors) use other columns, which helps when missingness is related to those columns, and they are the ones that will not finish comfortably on a full NFIP-scale file on a laptop. Every single completed table still treats guesses as if they had been observed, so standard errors from that table are too small; multiple imputation is the fix for that uncertainty, and scikit-learn does not pool it for you.

### Cited Findings

#### Listwise deletion (complete-case analysis)

- Listwise deletion drops every row that is missing on any analysis variable. If the data are missing completely at random (MCAR), it gives unbiased means, variances, and regression weights, with standard errors that are valid for the reduced sample and usually larger than an efficient use of all rows. If the data are not MCAR, it can severely bias means, regression coefficients, and correlations. The bias in a mean grows with the gap between observed and missing cases and with the fraction missing. It is often wasteful: with many columns, a large share of rows can disappear. It is not always worse than imputation; whether it is acceptable depends on whether the missingness is in the outcome or the predictors and on the complete-data model. — [van Buuren, Flexible Imputation of Missing Data, §1.3.1](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)
- Little and Rubin (2002, pp. 41–44) are cited there for the mean-bias result, and Schafer and Graham (2002) for bias of listwise deletion under missing at random (MAR) and missing not at random (MNAR). — [van Buuren, §1.3.1](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)
- In van Buuren's summary table, listwise deletion is unbiased for the mean, the regression weight, and the correlation only under MCAR, and its standard errors are too large relative to methods that keep incomplete rows. — [van Buuren, Table 1.1](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)

#### Pairwise deletion (available-case analysis)

- Pairwise deletion computes each mean from the rows observed on that variable, and each covariance from the rows observed on that pair, then feeds the summary matrices to a model. Under MCAR it is consistent for means, correlations, and covariances (Little and Rubin 2002, p. 55). It can be biased if the data are not MCAR. The covariance matrix may not be positive definite, which breaks many multivariate procedures; the problem is worse when variables are highly correlated (Little 1992). It is unclear which sample size to use for standard errors; using the average sample size makes them too small (Little 1992). It assumes numeric variables and an approximately normal distribution, which mixed claim files do not have. van Buuren does not recommend it outside low-correlation, roughly normal, MCAR problems. — [van Buuren, §1.3.2](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)

#### Mean

- Mean imputation replaces every missing entry in a column with that column's observed mean. It is fast. It underestimates the variance, disturbs relationships, biases almost any estimate other than the mean, and biases the mean itself when the data are not MCAR. In the airquality illustration, imputing the mean of Ozone created a visible spike, cut the standard deviation from 33 to 28.7, and dropped the Ozone–Solar.R correlation from 0.35 to 0.30. van Buuren's advice is to use it only as a rapid fix when a handful of values are missing, and to avoid it in general. The mode is the analogous fill for categorical data. — [van Buuren, §1.3.3](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)
- In scikit-learn 1.9.1, `SimpleImputer(strategy="mean")` does this per column and can only be used with numeric data. The default strategy is `"mean"`. — [SimpleImputer](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.impute.SimpleImputer.html)
- In van Buuren's summary table, mean imputation is unbiased for the mean only under MCAR, is not unbiased for regression weights or correlations, and produces standard errors that are too small. — [van Buuren, Table 1.1](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)

#### Median

- `SimpleImputer(strategy="median")` replaces missing numeric values with the column median. It cannot be used on non-numeric data. — [SimpleImputer](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.impute.SimpleImputer.html)
- A box-and-whisker plot, which Tukey introduced, summarizes the center by the median and the middle half by the box, and separates far points from that box. That is the display that stays readable when a mean would be pulled by extreme values. — [Tukey EDA overview, Utah State](https://www.usu.edu/math/schneit/StatsHistory/ModernStatisticians/Tukey); [Statgraphics on Tukey's five-number boxplot](https://www.statgraphics.com/exploratory-data-analysis)
- No primary source in this pass gave a theorem that median imputation is unbiased. The scikit-learn parameter list only defines the operation.

#### Mode (`most_frequent`)

- `strategy="most_frequent"` fills with the most frequent value and works for strings or numbers. If several values tie, only the smallest is returned. — [SimpleImputer](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.impute.SimpleImputer.html)
- van Buuren treats the mode as the categorical analogue of the mean fill. — [van Buuren, §1.3.3](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)

#### Constant fill and structural zero

- `strategy="constant"` replaces missing entries with `fill_value`. For numeric data, `fill_value=None` becomes 0; for object or string data it becomes the string `"missing_value"`. Added as a named strategy in scikit-learn 0.20. Since version 1.5, `strategy` may also be a callable that returns a scalar from the non-missing values. — [SimpleImputer](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.impute.SimpleImputer.html)
- If `keep_empty_features=False` (the default), a column that is entirely missing at `fit` is dropped on `transform`. If `keep_empty_features=True`, that column is kept and filled with 0, unless `strategy="constant"`, in which case `fill_value` is used. — [SimpleImputer](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.impute.SimpleImputer.html)
- Actuarial models of total loss often need a point mass at zero plus a separate positive severity distribution. Zero-adjusted lognormal, gamma, or inverse Gaussian distributions put probability at zero for policies with no loss and a continuous distribution on the positive amounts. The probability of a zero can depend on covariates, unlike a Tweedie model in which that probability cannot. — [Klein, Lang, and others, nonlife ratemaking discussion paper](https://cdn.uclouvain.be/public/Exports%20reddot/stat/documents/DP2013_45_ALL.pdf)
- Claim severity is described as right-skewed and often heavy-tailed; frequency data commonly have extra zeros that a plain Poisson or a plain Gaussian model does not represent. Total claim cost is described as typically zero-inflated and right-skewed, so normal-error models are a poor match. — [Počuča et al. abstract, Insurance: Mathematics and Economics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167668720300846); [neural frequency-severity model, arXiv:2106.10770](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.10770v2)

#### Explicit missing category

- For string or object columns, `SimpleImputer(strategy="constant", fill_value=...)` can fill with a chosen label. The docs' default string, if `fill_value` is omitted, is `"missing_value"`. — [SimpleImputer](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.impute.SimpleImputer.html)
- This pass did not find a textbook result that an extra "missing" level is unbiased for category proportions. See the indicator-method findings below for the related regression case.

#### Missingness indicator

- `MissingIndicator` builds a boolean mask of missing entries. Default `features="missing-only"` emits a column only for features that had missing values during `fit`. Default `error_on_new=True` raises if `transform` sees missing values in a feature that was complete at `fit`. `SimpleImputer` and `IterativeImputer` both have `add_indicator=False` by default; setting it `True` stacks this mask on the imputed output. A feature with no missing values at fit time does not get an indicator even if the test set has missing values there. The docs say the indicator should not be dropped into a plain `Pipeline` of transformers plus a classifier by itself; use a `FeatureUnion` or `ColumnTransformer`. — [MissingIndicator](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.impute.MissingIndicator.html); [sklearn imputation user guide, §8.4.6](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/impute.html)
- The older "indicator method" fills the hole (classically with zero, or with a mean) and adds the response indicator to a regression. It keeps the full sample and can pick up systematic differences between observed and unobserved rows. Under a narrow set of trial conditions (missingness only in a baseline covariate, interest only in a treatment effect, perfect compliance, linear model without interactions) it can estimate that treatment effect without bias even if missingness depends on the covariate. Outside those conditions it is not a general fix: it cannot handle a missing outcome, and it can severely bias regression estimates even under MCAR and even when little data are missing (Vach and Blettner 1991; Greenland and Finkle 1995; Knol et al. 2010). van Buuren's table marks the indicator method as not unbiased for the mean, the regression weight, or the correlation, with standard errors that are too small. — [van Buuren, §1.3.7 and Table 1.1](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)

#### Group-wise (stratified) median or mode

- No scikit-learn imputer in the 1.9.1 imputation guide computes a median or mode inside groups. `SimpleImputer` statistics are column-wide. — [sklearn imputation user guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/impute.html)
- Hot-deck adjustment cells are the surveyed analogue: donors are drawn inside classes defined by covariates. Cells that are homogeneous in both the outcome and the probability of responding reduce nonresponse bias, but only to the extent that the class variables are associated with both. Over-stratifying adds noise. Finding good matches is more realistic in large samples than in small ones. — [Andridge and Little, review of hot deck imputation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3130338/)

#### Regression imputation

- Regression imputation fits a model on observed rows and writes the fitted prediction into the hole. The filled values vary less than the real data. In the airquality example the imputed points lie on a line (correlation 1 among themselves) and the combined Ozone–Solar.R correlation rises from 0.35 to 0.39 at about 24% missing. Correlations are biased upward. Variability is underestimated by an amount that depends on the explained variance and the fraction missing (Little and Rubin 2002, p. 64). Means are unbiased under MCAR. Regression weights of the imputation model are unbiased under MCAR if the predictors are complete, and under MAR if the factors that drive missingness are in the model. van Buuren calls deterministic regression imputation, including machine-learning versions that still write a single prediction, a way to manufacture relationships that are too strong and to produce false-positive associations. — [van Buuren, §1.3.4](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)
- A worked numeric illustration of the same pattern: mean imputation shrank a slope, a correlation, and a standard deviation; deterministic regression imputation preserved the slope and inflated the correlation; only adding a residual brought the spread and the correlation back near the complete-data values. That page is a secondary exposition, not Little and Rubin. — [Tidy Ecology, single imputation bias and variance](https://tidyecology.com/posts/single-imputation-bias-variance/)

#### Stochastic regression

- Stochastic regression adds a random residual to the prediction so the filled cloud has scatter. It can preserve regression weights and correlations when the model is right, and it is the idea behind later imputation methods. It still fails in ordinary ways on non-negative, heteroscedastic data: the airquality example produced a negative imputed ozone value, and the residual draw assumed constant spread while the observed cloud was cone-shaped, so the high end was not covered. A linear model for a non-negative variable can predict negatives even before noise is added. — [van Buuren, §1.3.5](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)
- In the summary table, stochastic regression can be unbiased for the mean, the regression weight, and the correlation under MAR if the model is correct, but the standard error is still too small because there is only one completed dataset. — [van Buuren, Table 1.1](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)
- In scikit-learn 1.9.1, `IterativeImputer(sample_posterior=True)` draws from the Gaussian predictive posterior of the fitted estimator. The estimator must support `return_std` in `predict`. The default estimator is `BayesianRidge()`. Early stopping is applied only when `sample_posterior=False`. The docs say to set `sample_posterior=True` if using `IterativeImputer` for multiple imputations. — [IterativeImputer](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.impute.IterativeImputer.html)

#### Predictive mean matching

- Predictive mean matching (PMM) predicts the target, finds a small pool of observed cases whose predictions are closest (van Buuren says typically 3, 5, or 10; the mice default he states is 5), and copies one of those observed values at random. Imputations are values that already occur, so they stay inside the observed range and do not produce impossible numbers such as a negative height. The method is an implicit model (Little and Rubin 2002): it does not need an explicit distribution for the missing values. It is fairly robust to transforming the target, so imputing log(Y) often looks similar to imputing on the original scale, and it allows discrete targets. It works best in large samples. It cannot extrapolate beyond the observed range, and it cannot interpolate through a sparse gap inside that range. It can do poorly in small datasets. — [van Buuren, §3.4](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-pmm.html)
- Kleinke (2017), as summarized by van Buuren, found that PMM worked across many settings but that the default does not handle severe skewness or small samples. A simulation summarized there concluded PMM "may be the preferred approach" when under 50% of cases are missing and the missingness is not MNAR (Marshall et al. 2010). Marshall, Altman, and Holder (2010) are also cited for least bias and better performance measures in a missing-covariate setting. — [van Buuren, §3.4.5](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-pmm.html)
- Kleinke's own abstract: PMM generally worked unless the data were highly skewed and more than about 20% to 30% had to be imputed, small donor pools beat large ones, and larger samples helped. — [Kleinke, Journal of Educational and Behavioral Statistics](https://doi.org/10.3102/1076998616687084)
- In mice, `pmm` is the default univariate method for continuous data. Other built-ins include `cart` and `rf` for mixed types, `norm.predict` for deterministic normal predictions, and `norm` for Bayesian linear regression. — [van Buuren, §6.3.1](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-modelform.html)
- A clinical tutorial states the same contrast: drawing from a normal linear model can impute negative values for a positive variable and misses nonlinearity; PMM copies an observed neighbor in predicted-mean space. Morris et al. are cited for a pool of 10. PMM preserves discreteness, avoids impossible values, preserves quantile locations, and is robust to a misspecified imputation model. Means and regression coefficients, which depend on the middle of the distribution, may look similar under a careful parametric model and under PMM. — [Austin et al., PMC tutorial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8499698/)
- The scikit-learn 1.9.1 imputation user guide, as retrieved, documents `SimpleImputer`, `IterativeImputer`, `KNNImputer`, and `MissingIndicator`. It does not document a PMM class. — [sklearn imputation user guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/impute.html)

#### Hot deck

- Hot deck replaces each missing value with an observed response from a similar unit. It yields a rectangular file, imputes only values that were actually observed, avoids fitting an explicit parametric model for the target, and can reduce nonresponse bias when the variables that define the donor pool are associated with both response and the target. The similarity metric is still an assumption. Adjustment cells limit how many covariates you can use before cells empty out. Good matches are more likely in large samples. There is no consensus on the best way to do inference from one hot-deck file; uncertainty has to be carried by a replicate method or by multiple imputation. — [Andridge and Little](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3130338/)
- Drawing donors with probability proportional to sampling weights, or weighting inside the cell, did not remove bias in a simulation when the outcome was related to the sampling weight and response propensity still varied inside the cell. The review's recommendation is to put the design variables into the cell definition rather than to reweight the draw. — [Andridge and Little on weights in the hot deck](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3117228/)

#### k-nearest neighbors

- `KNNImputer` (scikit-learn 1.9.1) fills each missing feature with the mean of `n_neighbors` nearest training rows (default 5). Closeness uses features observed on both rows. The default metric is `nan_euclidean`. Weights are `"uniform"` or `"distance"`. If several features are missing, the neighbor set can differ by feature. If fewer than `n_neighbors` donors exist and there is no defined distance to the training set, the training-set mean for that feature is used. A feature that is always missing in training is removed during `transform` unless empty-feature handling says otherwise. The user guide points to Troyanskaya et al. (2001) for the method. — [KNNImputer](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.impute.KNNImputer.html); [sklearn user guide §8.4.4](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/impute.html)
- `KNeighborsRegressor` inside `IterativeImputer` is not the same algorithm: ordinary KNN regression does not use a distance that ignores missing coordinates, whereas `KNNImputer` does. — [sklearn example, IterativeImputer variants](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/impute/plot_iterative_imputer_variants_comparison.html)
- Because the imputer stores the training matrix and searches neighbors, this pass found no evidence it is practical for hundreds of thousands of rows on a laptop. No timing on an NFIP-sized file was found.

#### Iterative imputation / MICE, including Bayesian ridge and random-forest backends

- MICE (multivariate imputation by chained equations) specifies a univariate imputation model for each incomplete variable and iterates. Fully conditional specification does not require the analyst to write one joint likelihood, but it assumes a joint distribution exists so that the conditionals are coherent. Categorical imputation in mice was extended to reduce perfect-prediction failures. — [van Buuren and Groothuis-Oudshoorn, Journal of Statistical Software](https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v045i03); [Stekhoven and Bühlmann on fully conditional specification](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/28/1/112/219101)
- `IterativeImputer` models each incomplete column from the others in round-robin fashion, for up to `max_iter` rounds (default 10). It stops early, when `sample_posterior=False`, once the largest absolute change relative to the largest absolute observed value falls below `tol` (default 0.001). Default estimator is `BayesianRidge()`. `initial_strategy` defaults to `"mean"` and accepts `"median"`, `"most_frequent"`, and `"constant"`. `min_value` and `max_value` default to −inf and +inf, so out-of-range predictions are not clipped unless the student sets them. `n_nearest_features` can limit the predictors, drawn with probability proportional to absolute correlation after the initial fill, which the docs say can speed up wide data. The estimator is still experimental: predictions and the API can change with no deprecation cycle. Use requires `from sklearn.experimental import enable_iterative_imputer`. Open issues cited for stabilization are convergence criteria (#14338) and default estimators (#13286). — [IterativeImputer](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.impute.IterativeImputer.html); [user guide §8.4.3](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/impute.html)
- On one California-housing pattern (one value removed at random per row), `BayesianRidge` and `RandomForestRegressor` were the best of the estimators compared. The same example says `IterativeImputer` with a random forest can mimic missForest, and that `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` can use missing values natively and is often preferable to a heavy imputation pipeline. — [sklearn IterativeImputer variants example](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/impute/plot_iterative_imputer_variants_comparison.html)
- A random-forest backend writes predictions averaged over trees. That is deterministic regression imputation unless a residual or a posterior draw is added. `sample_posterior=True` is only valid for estimators whose `predict` accepts `return_std`. This pass did not open the `RandomForestRegressor.predict` page; the IterativeImputer contract implies a forest regressor does not qualify unless a wrapper adds `return_std`.

#### missForest

- missForest iteratively fits a random forest to the observed part of each variable and predicts the missing part. It handles continuous and categorical variables together, including interactions and nonlinear relations, without a parametric distribution. The 2012 paper reported lower imputation error than k-nearest neighbors and than default MICE in several biological datasets with 10–30% MCAR holes, especially when interactions and nonlinearity were plausible. Out-of-bag (OOB) error (normalized RMSE for numeric variables, proportion falsely classified for factors) tracked the true imputation error, with a stated average proportional deviation of no more than about 10–15%, and tended to underestimate error as the missingness fraction grew. The paper says averaging over trees "intrinsically constitutes a multiple imputation scheme," and in the same paper distinguishes that from MICE, which exists to assess uncertainty and to pool imputations. The released object is one completed matrix plus an OOB error, not a set of imputations with Rubin's rules. — [Stekhoven and Bühlmann, Bioinformatics 2012](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/28/1/112/219101)
- The R package missForest 1.6.1 (date 2025-10-22, published on CRAN 2025-10-26) still does this. The default backend is now ranger, with a legacy randomForest backend. It reports OOB NRMSE and PFC and can run in parallel. — [missForest CRAN reference](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/missForest/refman/missForest.html); [vignette](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/missForest/vignettes/missForest_1.6.html)
- The 2012 timing table found KNNimpute faster than missForest, and missForest faster than MICE, on the small biological matrices in that study. Those matrices are not hundreds of thousands of claim rows. The abstract's phrase "attractive computational efficiency" refers to that comparison. — [Stekhoven and Bühlmann](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/28/1/112/219101)

#### Single versus multiple imputation

- Single imputation cannot represent uncertainty about which value to impute. Analyses that treat imputed values as observed systematically underestimate uncertainty, even if the nonresponse mechanism is known. They also cannot represent uncertainty about the mechanism itself. — [Rubin, multiple imputation overview, Statistics Canada reprint](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/12-001-x/1986001/article/14439-eng.pdf)
- Little and Rubin: imputations should be draws from a predictive distribution. An important limitation of the single-imputation methods they review is that standard sampling-variance formulas applied to the filled-in data systematically underestimate the true sampling variance, even if the imputation model is correct. They contrast filling in the center of the predictive distribution with drawing from it. — [Little and Rubin, Statistical Analysis with Missing Data, chapter on single imputation, Wiley abstract](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119482260.ch4)
- NCES, citing Rubin (1978) and Kovar and Whitridge: when standard formulas are used on imputed data, variances of means and totals are underestimated because software treats imputations as observed. The cited review gives about 2 to 10 percent underestimation at 5 percent nonresponse and as much as 10 to 50 percent at 30 percent nonresponse, varying by imputation method. Multiple imputation estimates the extra variance by repeating the draw and using the between-replicate variance. The same appendix quotes Little and Rubin (1987) that nonresponse bias is often more important than variance bias, and that a valid variance around a badly biased estimate can be worse than no variance estimate. — [NCES Statistical Standards, Appendix B](https://www.nces.ed.gov/statprog/2002/appendixb4.asp)
- scikit-learn's `IterativeImputer` returns one array. `sample_posterior=True` is documented as the switch "if using IterativeImputer for multiple imputations," which means the student must refit or redraw with different randomness and pool the analyses outside scikit-learn. This pass did not find a Rubin's-rules helper in the imputation user guide. — [IterativeImputer](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.impute.IterativeImputer.html)

#### What the notebook should show, by method

These checks are the visual consequence of the findings above. They are not extra methods.

- Deletion: a table of rows kept versus rows dropped, overall and by year, plus the mean and median of the payment on the full observed column versus the complete-case subset. A large shift means the dropped rows are not a random slice.
- Pairwise: if correlations are shown, print the pairwise complete count next to each pair. Do not silently compare correlations that were computed on different row sets.
- Mean, median, mode, constant: a histogram with observed and filled values in different colors, following van Buuren's blue/red figure, plus a three-number table (mean, median, variance) before and after. The spike at the fill value is the finding.
- Structural zero versus constant zero: a bar of the share of exact zeros before any fill, computed only on rows where the amount was observed. Filling missing with 0 changes that share; the notebook should show the share before and after.
- Explicit missing category: a bar of levels with the missing label drawn separately from real codes, and a count of how much the most common real level grew.
- Indicator: a small table or bar of the missing rate. For prediction, the indicator is a feature that must be fit with `MissingIndicator` on training rows only. For a regression coefficient, van Buuren's citations say not to treat the indicator method as a general unbiased fix.
- Group-wise median or mode: cell counts beside the within-group statistic. Cells with few observed rows should fall back to a coarser statistic, and the notebook should count how often that fallback fires.
- Regression and stochastic regression: a scatter of imputed versus a predictor, with imputed points marked. Deterministic regression puts them on a curve. Stochastic regression spreads them and can cross 0 or a coverage cap; count those impossible values.
- PMM and hot deck: a table of how many distinct donor values were used and the maximum times any donor was reused. Imputed values should be a subset of observed values. PMM should be tried on a sample if the student implements it; it is not in scikit-learn 1.9.1's impute module.
- k-nearest neighbors: the same before/after histogram on a sample small enough to run. Expect new numeric values that never occur in the data, because neighbors are averaged. Do not run it on the full claims file until a sample has been timed.
- Iterative imputation: record `initial_strategy`, `min_value`, `max_value`, the estimator name, and the number of rounds. Bayesian ridge is the default and is the laptop-scale multivariate option to try first, on a column subset or a row sample. A random-forest backend is the missForest-style option and is the one to treat as too heavy for hundreds of thousands of rows times many columns times several iterations.
- missForest: if the student uses R, report the OOB NRMSE or PFC as an error estimate, and still show the before/after distribution. Do not describe the OOB number as a confidence interval for a mean payment.
- Single versus multiple: for a course model, one imputation inside a pipeline is the practical artifact. For any interval around a mean or a coefficient, the notebook should say that one completed file omits between-imputation variance. A cheap sensitivity check is two or three stochastic fills (different seeds) and the range of the resulting mean.

### Inferences

- Median imputation has the same spike-and-shrunk-variance geometry as mean imputation. The spike sits at a more typical dollar amount when the column is right-skewed, which is why it is the better univariate default for claim sizes, not because it restores the distribution.
- Mode imputation makes the most common code more common and makes rare codes rarer. That is the distortion that matters for rare events. Tie-breaking toward the smallest value can also prefer a low code or a low number for no substantive reason.
- A constant 0 is appropriate only when missing truly means "none." Using it because 0 is the numeric default mixes a real point mass at zero with rows whose amount was never recorded. `keep_empty_features=True` turning an all-missing column into 0 has the same danger.
- An explicit missing level is a good EDA and prediction device when the fact of missingness is known at prediction time and is informative. It is not a claim that the missing rows have a real code.
- Group-wise medians help when a coarse group (year, state, occupancy) has a very different typical amount and missingness is roughly random inside the group. Stratifying on the payment itself, or on a variable built from the payment, copies the target into the feature.
- Deterministic machine-learning imputation, including a random forest that returns the average of trees, inherits van Buuren's warning about regression imputation: relationships look cleaner than they are. missForest's OOB error measures reconstruction error, not the extra variance of a scientific estimate.
- PMM and hot deck are the methods that automatically respect a coverage cap and a point mass at zero, because they can only copy observed dollars. They fail when almost everyone in a region of the predictors is missing, because the donor pool is then elsewhere in the distribution.
- For a laptop course project the feasible stack is `SimpleImputer` (median, most frequent, or constant), an optional missingness indicator, and group-wise medians implemented with pandas on training rows. `IterativeImputer` with `BayesianRidge` is the step up, on a sample or a few columns. `KNNImputer`, random-forest `IterativeImputer`, and R `missForest` should be demonstrated on a sample and not presented as the full-file pipeline unless a timing run says otherwise.
- Multiple imputation is the right method when the graded product is an interval for a mean or a regression coefficient. It is optional overhead when the graded product is a predictive model, as long as the write-up says the scores ignore imputation uncertainty.

### Gaps

- No source in this pass stated a 2026 recommended number of imputations. Older rules of thumb were not re-quoted from the book page.
- No maintained Python package named missForest was verified. The verified implementation is the R package 1.6.1. The scikit-learn example only says a random-forest `IterativeImputer` can mimic it.
- No timing numbers were found for `KNNImputer` or `IterativeImputer` at NFIP row counts.
- `RandomForestRegressor.predict` was not opened to confirm it lacks `return_std`; that limit is inferred from the `IterativeImputer` contract.
- pandas group-by median syntax was not re-fetched. Stratified fills are described from the hot-deck literature plus the absence of a grouped strategy on `SimpleImputer`.

## How do right-skew, zero inflation, coverage caps, and outliers change mean versus median, and raw versus log1p?

### Takeaway

On claim dollars, the mean is a tail summary and the median is the resistant center, so a median fill distorts a skewed column less than a mean fill. Neither fill repairs the shape. `log1p` is for seeing and modeling the positive tail; it does not remove the pile of zeros, and a mean computed on the log scale is not the mean of the dollars. Statutory caps create a second spike. Donor methods stay inside observed payments, including the cap; linear and stochastic regression can invent negative payments and payments above the cap unless they are clipped or the student switches to predictive mean matching.

### Cited Findings

- Mean imputation underestimates variance and biases quantities other than the mean. The airquality figure shows a new mode at the imputed mean. — [van Buuren, §1.3.3](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)
- Tukey's boxplot is built from the median and the quartiles so that extreme points do not set the center. — [Utah State on Tukey](https://www.usu.edu/math/schneit/StatsHistory/ModernStatisticians/Tukey); [Statgraphics](https://www.statgraphics.com/exploratory-data-analysis)
- Insurance total loss is modeled with a point mass at zero plus a positive skewed severity distribution (zero-adjusted lognormal, gamma, or inverse Gaussian). Severity itself is modeled on the positive amounts with lognormal, gamma, or inverse Gaussian, which is a log-scale or heavy-tail choice rather than a raw-scale Gaussian. — [Klein, Lang, and others](https://cdn.uclouvain.be/public/Exports%20reddot/stat/documents/DP2013_45_ALL.pdf)
- Published descriptions of insurance loss say the severity distribution is right-skewed, often heavy-tailed or multimodal, and that frequency has extra zeros. A neural frequency-severity paper uses a French auto portfolio as the example of a target that is mostly zeros with a long right tail, and says ordinary normal-error models do not fit that shape. — [Počuča et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167668720300846); [arXiv:2106.10770](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.10770v2)
- NFIP regular-program maximums published by FEMA include $250,000 building and $100,000 contents for a single-family home, a residential manufactured/mobile home, a residential condominium unit, and a two-to-four-family building. Other residential buildings are listed at $500,000 building and $100,000 contents. Non-residential buildings are listed at $500,000 building and $500,000 contents. The FAQ table is dated as regular-program limits as of April 2022 in that brochure PDF. These are purchase caps, not typical payments. — [FEMA, Answers to Questions About the NFIP (brochure PDF, coverage question)](https://www.agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/fema-answers-to-questions-about-nfip-brochure-06-2023.pdf); [FloodSmart single-family guide](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/nfip-risk-rating-2.0-simple-guide-single-family-homes.pdf)
- The October 2021 Flood Insurance Manual excerpt also shows emergency-program maxima far below those regular-program numbers (for example $35,000 building and $10,000 contents for a single-family home). Caps are not one pair of constants for every policy. — [NFIP Flood Insurance Manual excerpt, October 2021, Tables 22 and 23](https://pw.lacounty.gov/wmd/NFIP/documents/LOMR/Std%20Ins%20Coverge%20and%20Deducts%20NFIP%20Fld%20Insur%20Manual%20Oct%202021.pdf)
- Stochastic linear imputation can produce values below zero on a non-negative variable, and it assumes constant residual spread. — [van Buuren, §1.3.5](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)
- PMM copies an observed value, so it does not invent values outside the observed range, and van Buuren reports that it is fairly robust to whether the target is imputed on the log scale or the original scale. Performance degrades under severe skew combined with a high missingness fraction. — [van Buuren, §3.4](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-pmm.html); [Kleinke](https://doi.org/10.3102/1076998616687084)
- `IterativeImputer` will leave predictions outside any business range unless `min_value` and `max_value` are set; both default to infinity. — [IterativeImputer](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.impute.IterativeImputer.html)
- OpenFEMA's claims data page documents that some paid amounts can be negative when a check is reissued. A log or `log1p` transform is undefined for negative inputs. — [OpenFEMA, FIMA NFIP Redacted Claims v2, amount-paid field notes](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/fima-nfip-redacted-claims-v2)
- seaborn 0.13.2 `histplot(..., log_scale=True)` log-transforms the data and bins in log space. That is not the same as drawing a histogram of already-transformed numbers, and it is not `log1p`. The author of seaborn distinguished it from matplotlib, which can bin in linear space and only then change the axis scale. — [seaborn.histplot](https://seaborn.pydata.org/generated/seaborn.histplot.html); [mwaskom, seaborn issue 3579](https://github.com/mwaskom/seaborn/issues/3579)

### Inferences

- Prefer the median over the mean as a univariate fill for a payment column because outliers and the mass near a coverage cap pull the mean above a typical paid claim. Show both numbers on the chart so the gap is visible. Use the mean later if the question is total dollars, and label it as a total, not as a typical claim.
- Zero inflation changes the median in a specific way: if more than half of observed values are zero, the median is zero, and median imputation fills every hole with zero. That silently converts missing into "no payment." The notebook should print the share of zeros before choosing the median.
- A coverage cap adds a point mass at a legal maximum. The mean moves toward that cap when large events hit the limit. The median usually does not. Imputed values above the cap that applies to that policy are not plausible payments. PMM and hot deck avoid them. Bayesian ridge and stochastic regression do not, unless `min_value`/`max_value` or a post-check is used. Clipping at the cap piles still more mass on the cap and should be counted.
- `log1p` is the right scale for a histogram of non-negative amounts because `log1p(0)` is defined and the positive tail spreads out. It does not fix zero inflation: the zeros remain a spike at 0 on the `log1p` axis. It is the wrong tool for negative payments; those rows have to be counted and set aside before the transform.
- Imputing on the `log1p` scale with a mean, then returning with `expm1`, does not reproduce the arithmetic mean of dollars. The exponential of an average log is a different functional. For a median, the back-transform of the median of `log1p` is the median of the dollars, because the median commutes with a monotone transform. That is a reason to median-impute on the raw scale, or to median-impute on `log1p` and back-transform, and a reason not to mean-impute on `log1p` and present the result as a dollar mean.
- seaborn's `log_scale=True` drops or breaks zeros. For this file, build an explicit `log1p` column of the non-negative amounts and plot that on a linear axis, and plot a second histogram of positive amounts only.
- Severe skew plus a large missingness fraction is exactly where Kleinke found PMM weakening. A student who uses PMM should still show the before/after histogram rather than assume robustness.
- Do not quote $250,000 and $100,000 as the only caps in the file. They are the regular-program residential figures FEMA publishes for several occupancies. Other occupancies and the emergency program use other maxima. A spike in a histogram needs to be matched to the cap that applies to the rows in that plot, or labeled as "a statutory maximum," not annotated as if every policy shared one ceiling.

### Gaps

- This pass did not find an actuarial paper that estimates the bias of mean versus median imputation specifically for censored insurance payments. The cap argument is the combination of the published maxima and the general imputation results.
- Retransformation bias formulas (for example a smearing estimator) were not pulled from a primary paper in this pass. The notes state only the median-versus-mean distinction under a monotone transform.
- No measurement of how large the NFIP cap spike is appears here, on purpose: that is a computation on the claims file, not a literature fact.

## Why does imputing before a train/test or year split leak, and what should the notebook say?

### Takeaway

The filled value is a statistic of the rows used to fit the imputer. If those rows include the test set, or later years in a time split, the training features contain information from the data used to judge the model. Fit the imputer on the training period only, and apply that frozen statistic to the later period. An EDA notebook may impute for a picture, and it should say that the picture is not the modeling pipeline.

### Cited Findings

- scikit-learn defines leakage as using information that would not be available at prediction time, which makes cross-validation scores too optimistic. Preprocessing must be learned on the training subset only. The user guide's example is a normalization: the average used to scale the data must be the training average, not the average of all the data. It names `SimpleImputer` alongside `StandardScaler` and `PCA` as a transformer with this risk. The stated practice is to split first, call `fit` or `fit_transform` only on the training subset, call `transform` on the test subset, and prefer a `Pipeline` so cross-validation refits the transformer inside each training fold. — [scikit-learn 1.9.1, common pitfalls, §12.2](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/common_pitfalls.html)
- The same point in a teaching note: filling missing values in pandas on the whole frame, then handing the frame to scikit-learn, uses both the training and the test rows to choose the fill. During cross-validation the training rows change every fold, so a single pandas fill cannot be refit per fold. A transformer inside a pipeline is refit on each fold's training rows. — [Data School, leakage in pandas and scikit-learn](https://www.dataschool.io/machine-learning-data-leakage/)
- `MissingIndicator(error_on_new=True)` and `add_indicator` only create indicator columns for features that were missing at fit time. A leak-safe pipeline still has a defined behavior when a later year is missing a column the training years always observed: the default is to raise, not to invent a new indicator from the test year. — [MissingIndicator](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.impute.MissingIndicator.html)

### Inferences

- A random row split and a split by year leak in the same way. The year split is the one that matches a claims file, because a later hurricane season should not help decide the median that fills an earlier season if the model is being scored as a forecast.
- Group-wise medians leak if the group statistic is computed on all years. Compute it on the training years, and decide in advance what to do with a group that appears only later (fall back to the training global median).
- k-nearest neighbors leaks more richly than a median: every test row is filled from training neighbors, but if the imputer was fit on all rows, neighbors include other test claims, including claims from the same event.
- Iterative imputation leaks through every column used as a predictor, not only through the column being filled.
- The EDA notebook should contain a short written caveat, not only a code comment: any imputation shown before a split is diagnostic; the modeling notebook must fit imputers only on training rows (or only on years up to the split) and must call `transform` on the held-out rows. A `Pipeline` is the way to keep that true inside cross-validation.
- It is legitimate for EDA to compute a median on the full extract in order to see the spike. That chart answers "what would this fill do to the distribution?" It does not answer "what score will a model get?"

### Gaps

- This pass did not find an NFIP-specific paper quantifying the optimism from pre-split imputation. The mechanism is the general one in the scikit-learn pitfalls guide.
- How large the leak is for a median, compared with k-nearest neighbors, was not measured.

## Which charts and tables does the notebook need, and which Python tools draw them?

### Takeaway

Use pandas for the counts and the before/after table, seaborn or matplotlib for distributions and top category bars, and missingno for the missingness bar, matrix, and nullity heatmap. Draw claim dollars on the raw scale and on a `log1p` scale, keep zeros and caps visible, and treat rounded coordinates as a grid, not as building locations. Sample the missingness matrix; do not paint every claim row.

### Cited Findings

- pandas is the table layer the other libraries sit on. missingno's examples take a pandas DataFrame. scikit-learn's imputers document pandas nullable integers and `pd.NA` (converted to `np.nan` for several imputers). — [missingno README](https://github.com/residentmario/missingno); [SimpleImputer](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.impute.SimpleImputer.html)
- `missingno.bar` is a column-wise bar of non-missing counts. `log=True` switches that bar to a log scale. `missingno.matrix` is a row-by-column nullity map with a sparkline of how many fields are present in each row; the README says it is comfortable up to about 50 labeled variables and shows a 250-row sample. For a datetime index, `freq` can mark time. `missingno.heatmap` is the correlation of the missingness indicators, from −1 (one present implies the other absent) to 1 (they are missing or present together). Columns that are always full or always empty are dropped because their missingness has no variance. The heatmap "has no particular support for extremely large datasets." A dendrogram is also documented. — [missingno README](https://github.com/residentmario/missingno); [CONFIGURATION.md](https://github.com/ResidentMario/missingno/blob/master/CONFIGURATION.md)
- The correlation in that heatmap is the Pearson correlation of the boolean missingness columns after dropping columns with zero missingness variance. — [missingno README, algorithm snippet](https://github.com/residentmario/missingno)
- PyPI listed missingno 0.5.2 at the time of this search. The release date was not on the snippet that was read. — [PyPI missingno](https://pypi.org/project/missingno/)
- seaborn 0.13.2 `histplot` draws a histogram and can show counts, proportions, or density. `boxplot` draws quartiles, whiskers at 1.5 times the interquartile range by default, and separate outlier points. Both accept `log_scale`, with the caveat above that this is a log transform, not `log1p`. — [seaborn.histplot](https://seaborn.pydata.org/generated/seaborn.histplot.html); [seaborn.boxplot](https://seaborn.pydata.org/generated/seaborn.boxplot.html)
- Tukey's exploratory displays exist to show range, where values concentrate, symmetry, gaps, and stray values. The stem-and-leaf keeps individual values; the boxplot compresses a batch to a five-number summary. For a claims file, the histogram and the boxplot are the practical descendants. A stem-and-leaf is not usable at this row count. — [Velleman and Hoaglin, ABCs of EDA, chapter 1, via Cornell eCommons](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/21d51b51-a50f-4418-b1d7-cd6e49907ef9/content); [Penn State list of EDA displays](https://online.stat.psu.edu/stat504/lesson/graphical-exploratory-data-analysis-eda)
- OpenFEMA describes latitude and longitude on the redacted claims file as approximate, to one decimal place, with precision reduced for privacy, and says a point may fall in the wrong county or state. Aggregation should use the state and county fields. — [OpenFEMA, FIMA NFIP Redacted Claims v2](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/fima-nfip-redacted-claims-v2)
- FEMA's NFIP data FAQ says coordinates are truncated to one decimal place, that mapped policies and claims may look clustered because of that anonymization, and that the coordinates are accurate to within approximately six miles. — [FloodSmart, FAQs about NFIP data](https://www.agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data)

#### Chart list

1. Row counts by year and by month. pandas `groupby` size, drawn as a seaborn or matplotlib bar for years and a year-by-month heatmap or line for seasonality. Purpose: see whether each year is complete, whether claim volume arrives in event spikes, and whether a month is empty because nothing was filed or because the extract was cut off. Fit imputers later only on the years chosen as training; this chart is what makes that split visible.

2. Missingness bar. `missingno.bar`, optionally `log=True` and `sort="descending"`. Purpose: rank columns by how much is observed. A handful of sparse columns will dominate listwise deletion; this bar shows that before any row is dropped.

3. Missingness matrix. `missingno.matrix` on a sample of rows, not the full extract. If the question is time, sort the sample by date or use a period index. Purpose: see blocks of fields that are missing on the same rows, and whether missingness changes across the file. The sparkline shows the row with the most holes.

4. Missingness correlation heatmap. `missingno.heatmap`. Purpose: which fields are missing together (correlation near 1) or as substitutes (near −1). Always-complete columns will vanish from this figure; say that in the caption. Do not read it as a correlation of the dollar amounts.

5. Duplicate counts. A pandas table, not a chart: number of rows that are exact copies, and number of repeated values of the project's claim identifier if one is defined. Purpose: decide whether duplicates would be imputed twice and then counted as two claims.

6. Numeric histograms and boxplots on the raw scale and on `log1p` of the non-negative values. `seaborn.histplot` and `seaborn.boxplot`. Purpose: the raw histogram shows the zero spike, the cap spike, and how little of the axis the typical claim occupies. The raw boxplot shows a median and a long upper tail; on a skewed payment it often looks crushed near the bottom. The `log1p` histogram shows the shape of the positive body. Plot positive amounts alone as a third panel so the zero spike does not set every bin. Do not use `log_scale=True` on a column that contains zeros.

7. Categorical bar charts of the top levels. pandas `value_counts` for the most common levels, plus an explicit "other" and an explicit "missing" bar, drawn with matplotlib or seaborn. Purpose: see concentration and rare codes without a chart of hundreds of levels. A companion table should list the number of distinct levels and how many rows sit in levels that appear only a few times.

8. Sentinel-value counts. A table, produced before those codes are turned into missing: for each column that uses special codes, the count of each code the project has decided is not a real measurement. Purpose: imputation only fills values marked missing. Codes still stored as numbers will be treated as real dollars or real categories.

9. Zero inflation. A two-bar or a one-line table: share of observed values that are exactly zero, share positive, and the count of negatives if any. Then the positive-only histogram from item 6. Purpose: decide whether a median fill would become a zero fill, and whether zeros are a real point mass rather than holes.

10. Target or payment distribution. The same raw and `log1p` histograms, with the mean and the median drawn as vertical lines, and with a line at a statutory cap only when the rows in the plot are subject to that cap. Purpose: show that the mean sits in the tail. This is the chart that justifies median rather than mean imputation for a typical-claim summary.

11. Before/after imputation comparison. A table of n, mean, median, and variance on the observed values only, and the same four numbers after each candidate fill. Pair it with an overlaid histogram that colors imputed rows differently from observed rows. Purpose: see the spike, the drop in variance, and whether the mean moved. van Buuren's mean-imputation figure is the template. — [van Buuren, Figure 1.2 discussion](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)

12. Geography, with the rounding caveat in the title of the figure. A state choropleth of claim counts, or of total paid dollars, built from the state field. A point map of latitude and longitude only as a diagnostic that the points sit on a 0.1-degree grid and stack. Caption: coordinates are truncated to one decimal place, are only accurate to within about six miles, can fall in the wrong county or state, and are not building locations. Prefer state or county fields for any aggregate. — [OpenFEMA claims page](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/fima-nfip-redacted-claims-v2); [FloodSmart FAQ](https://www.agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data)

Optional and still in the documented tools: `missingno` dendrogram if the heatmap is too crowded to see clusters of fields that are missing together. It is not a substitute for the heatmap's numeric correlations.

### Inferences

- Draw the missingness matrix on a sample of a few hundred to a few thousand rows, stratified by year so that early years are not accidentally omitted. The README's 250-row example is a display sample, not a statistical sample size.
- For categorical charts, plot at most the top levels. A chart of every code hides the rare-event question; the rare-event question belongs in a table of counts.
- A state choropleth of the mean payment will be dominated by the same skew and caps as the histogram. Counts, totals, or medians answer cleaner geographic questions. The point map does not get more honest if it is drawn with a smaller marker: many claims share one rounded coordinate.
- The Python stack to name in the notebook introduction is pandas, matplotlib, seaborn, and missingno, with scikit-learn's imputers used later inside a pipeline rather than as plotting tools.

### Gaps

- seaborn's `countplot` / `barplot` pages were not re-fetched. Categorical bars are attributed to matplotlib or seaborn generally; the verified seaborn pages are `histplot` and `boxplot`.
- pandas `duplicated` and `groupby` pages were not re-fetched.
- Whether missingno 0.5.2 was still the current release on 2026-10-20 was not confirmed beyond the PyPI page retrieved in this search.
- missingno's `geoplot` was named in the configuration notes and was not evaluated. It is not the recommended map for this file; the rounding caveat applies to any point map.

## What common EDA chart mistakes matter here?

### Takeaway

Three mistakes change the story of this file. The mean of a skewed payment is not a typical claim. A histogram drawn after imputation, with imputed rows painted the same color as observed rows, pretends the fill was data. A point map of one-decimal coordinates pretends those points are properties.

### Cited Findings

- Mean imputation changes the shape, shrinks the standard deviation, and attenuates correlations, even in a small MCAR-style textbook example (Ozone standard deviation 33 to 28.7; correlation 0.35 to 0.30). The imputed mass is a bar at the mean. — [van Buuren, §1.3.3](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)
- Deterministic regression imputation does the opposite to correlations: it strengthens them because filled points lie on the fitted curve. — [van Buuren, §1.3.4](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/sec-simplesolutions.html)
- Standard complete-data plots and variance formulas treat filled values as observed and therefore understate uncertainty. — [Little and Rubin, single-imputation chapter](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119482260.ch4); [Rubin, Statistics Canada reprint](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/12-001-x/1986001/article/14439-eng.pdf)
- Tukey-style EDA uses the median and the box, not the mean, to describe the center and the spread of a batch that may contain stray values. — [Velleman and Hoaglin, chapter 1](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/21d51b51-a50f-4418-b1d7-cd6e49907ef9/content)
- Total insurance loss is zero-inflated and right-skewed, so a normal model or a single mean is a poor summary of the distribution. — [arXiv:2106.10770](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.10770v2); [Klein, Lang, and others](https://cdn.uclouvain.be/public/Exports%20reddot/stat/documents/DP2013_45_ALL.pdf)
- Latitude and longitude on the redacted claims extract are to one decimal place, can plot in the wrong county or state, and look clustered because of anonymization. FEMA describes the precision as about six miles. — [OpenFEMA claims page](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/fima-nfip-redacted-claims-v2); [FloodSmart FAQ](https://www.agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data)
- `missingno.heatmap` correlates missingness indicators, not the data values, and it silently drops columns that are never missing. — [missingno README](https://github.com/residentmario/missingno)

### Inferences

- Annotating only the mean on a payment histogram, or putting the mean in the first results sentence, overstates the typical claim and understates how many claims are zero or small. The chart should show the median beside the mean, and the share of zeros.
- Imputing and then calling `histplot` on the completed column hides the spike inside a bin and makes variance look like a property of the claims. Keep a copy of the missingness mask and color the filled rows, or show the before/after table. Running missingno after the fill is useless: the missingness is gone.
- A scatter of rounded latitude and longitude, or a kernel density on those points, produces hot spots at grid intersections. Those hot spots are the rounding, not flood clusters. A state choropleth that ignores the "wrong state" warning can still be wrong for points near a border; the state field is the field FEMA says to aggregate.
- Other mistakes that follow from the same sources: a boxplot of raw payments with the default 1.5-IQR rule will flag a large share of ordinary large claims as outliers, because the rule assumes a distribution without this tail. A KDE drawn on a zero-inflated amount invents a smooth density over a point mass. A correlation heatmap of dollar columns after mean imputation shows weaker relationships than the observed rows, and after regression imputation shows stronger ones. A categorical bar with missing values dropped understates the incomplete level and inflates the shares of the levels that remain.

### Gaps

- No source quantified how often 1.5-IQR boxplot fences mis-label insurance severities. The warning is an inference from Tukey-style fences plus the actuarial description of skew.
- No source was found that measures how far a 0.1-degree point near a state border moves across that border. The OpenFEMA page already states that a point can fall in the wrong state; the notes do not add a distance calculation.
