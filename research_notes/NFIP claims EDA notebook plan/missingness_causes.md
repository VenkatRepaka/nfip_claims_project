# NFIP claims missingness causes

Scope: OpenFEMA NFIP Redacted Claims v3 (`NfipClaims`), U.S. losses with year of loss 2020–2025. Rates below that are not in a FEMA publication are from the assignment’s local extract. They are study facts, not citations, and must not be quoted as FEMA statistics.

Documented FEMA meaning and the Little–Rubin label are different layers. A blank is eligible for an MCAR / MAR / MNAR label only when a real value could have been recorded and was not. Structural inapplicability, a meaningful zero, a sentinel that encodes a real state, a retired field, and privacy suppression are not “missing data” in that sense.

Primary pages used: the v3 data-fields table at https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3 ; the NFIP data FAQ as published at https://agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data (the dataset page points at the PDF https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_nfip-data-faqs.pdf ; that PDF did not return text in this pass, and the FloodSmart page carries the same FAQ answers); the OpenFEMA program FAQ at https://www.fema.gov/about/openfema/faq ; the January 2015 consolidated TRRP Plan, Change 22, https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf ; TRRP Change 26 (effective 2016-10-01), https://nfipservices.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/trrp_change_26.pdf .

## What do the OpenFEMA NfipClaims v3 data dictionary and FEMA NFIP data FAQ say each empty value means?

### Takeaway

FEMA defines the empty value in plain language for only a few of these columns. Everywhere else the dictionary defines the filled value, and the blank has to be read from an older TRRP reporting rule, a sentinel printed in the dictionary, or a privacy note. The FAQ’s general rule is that the public file is what WYO companies reported, that “99XX” is a common placeholder when a required value was not reported, and that low-confidence fields were omitted rather than repaired.

### Cited Findings

Program-wide rules, not field-specific:

- The v3 file is “derived from the National Flood Insurance Program system of record … and redacted to protect policy holder personally identifiable information.” It is “not intended to be an official federal report.” — [NFIP Redacted Claims v3](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)
- “FEMA is reporting the data to the public as it was reported and recorded in the program’s System of Record.” Business rules “reject critical transaction errors but may allow for some minor errors.” “FEMA has omitted fields in the current OpenFEMA dataset for which there is low confidence.” Abnormal values “can at times persist” and then appear in OpenFEMA. — [FAQs About NFIP Data](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data)
- “Where our system requires a value but isn’t always reported, ‘99XX’ is often used as placeholder or default value.” Legacy reporting rules are the TRRP manuals, and “many of these definitions and practices still apply” after the 2019 PIVOT system of record. — [FAQs About NFIP Data](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data); same 99XX sentence on [OpenFEMA FAQ](https://www.fema.gov/about/openfema/faq)
- PII is redacted. Location is anonymized to census tract, reported ZIP, and latitude/longitude truncated to one decimal place, “accurate to within approximately six miles.” Street address, ZIP+4, census block, and full coordinates are withheld. Mapped points “may appear to be clustered” because of that anonymization. — [FAQs About NFIP Data](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data)
- The NFIP does not track claims by FEMA disaster declaration. It uses an Event Designation Number or a FICO number. “If an EDN was not assigned for a particular disaster,” claims are still found with state and a date-of-loss range. — [FAQs About NFIP Data](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data)
- DHS OIG: the legacy NFIP system allowed “duplicate records, missing data, and invalid entries (such as infeasible dates and values).” In October 2019 FIMA migrated “the vast majority of its historical legacy data, including errors,” into PIVOT. — [OIG-21-04](https://www.oig.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/assets/2020-11/OIG-21-04-Nov20.pdf)

Field by field. “Dictionary” means the v3 Data Fields table. “TRRP” means the 2015 consolidated plan unless Change 26 is named.

**100 percent null**

- `ficoNumber`. Dictionary: a number assigned to a significant flooding event by state. “EDN has replaced FICO as the new event tracking designation. Prior to June 2020 flood events were assigned FICO number designations; after June 2020, flood events are assigned EDN number designations.” The same note is on `eventDesignationNumber`. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). The column is still published. The dictionary does not say the column was dropped, redacted, or stored as a sentinel. It says the identifier was replaced for events after June 2020.
- `floodCharacteristicsIndicator`. Dictionary legal values: 1 velocity flow, 2 low-velocity flow or ponding, 3 wave action, 4 mudflow, 5 erosion. No sentence defines a blank. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). TRRP data element “Flood Characteristics” has the same five codes and “REPORTING REQUIREMENT: Optional.” Source: “the adjuster's report.” — [TRRP Plan, Flood Characteristics](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf). FEMA does not say the v3 column is redacted or retired.

**Above 90 percent empty**

- `floodWaterDuration`. Dictionary: “Number of hours flood water remained in the insured building.” No blank rule and no sentinel. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). TRRP “Duration of Flood Waters in the Building” is the same definition, positive numeric, length 3, “Information is obtained from the adjuster's report,” “REPORTING REQUIREMENT: Optional.” — [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf).
- `mostRecentRecoveryDate`. Dictionary: “Date of overpayment on building, contents, ICC, salvage or subrogation was recovered from insured.” — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). TRRP splits this idea. “Payment Recovery Date” is “the date a payment for a building claim, contents claim, or ICC claim was recovered.” “Actual Salvage Recovery Date” is “the date actual salvage recovery is made.” Both are “Required,” meaning required on the recovery transaction, not required on every claim. Transaction code `52A` is “Recovery After Final Payment.” — [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf). The dictionary does not say a blank date equals zero dollars.
- `totalSalvageRecovery` and the other recovery amounts (`totalBldgClaimPmtRecovery`, `totalContentsClaimPmtRecovery`, `totalIccClaimPmtRecovery`, `totalSubrogationRecovery`). Dictionary: salvage is “the monetary amount recovered from the sale or disposal of damaged property deemed salvageable after a loss”; building and contents recoveries are proceeds credited back after the insurer has paid the claim; ICC recovery is an ICC payment “issued in error” or paid when “eligibility were not met”; subrogation is “the monetary amount recovered by the NFIP from a third party.” — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). TRRP “Actual Salvage Recovery” is “a positive dollar amount when initially reported,” used when “subsequent to a claim settlement, the insurer sells damaged property and this recovery can be traced back to an individual claim.” Salvage handled by leaving the property with the insured and reducing the payment “is not reported in this field.” — [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf). Again, blank is not defined as zero. The reporting design records a recovery when one occurs.
- `elevationCertificateIndicator`. This is the one high-missing field whose blank FEMA defines directly. “An empty value indicates that this information was not reported; it does not necessarily mean that these properties do not have an Elevation Certificate, just that one was not reported at the time the policy was written.” Filled codes distinguish no certificate (1, 2), certificate with or without BFE (3, 4), and foundation diagrams (A–E). — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). Under Risk Rating 2.0, “An Elevation Certificate (EC) will no longer be required to purchase coverage.” FEMA uses its own elevation data. A policyholder “may choose to provide an EC.” ECs “continue to be used for floodplain management building requirements.” Phased implementation was 2021-10-01 through 2023-04-01. — [Risk Rating 2.0 FAQ](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/fema-nfip-risk-rating-2.0-FAQs.pdf); [FEMA pricing approach](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance/risk-rating). The renewal guide repeats that ECs “are no longer required to rate a policy” because FEMA can source ground elevation and first-floor height, and that an EC remains optional “for a potentially more favorable premium.” — [Risk Rating 2.0 renewal guide](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/fema-Risk-Rating-2.0-Renewal-Guide.pdf).

**Above 60 percent empty**

- `elevationDifference`. Dictionary: difference between lowest floor used for rating, or floodproofed elevation, and BFE or base flood depth. “The value of 9999.0 indicates the field is not reported and/or used for this policy.” — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). Older TRRP rating text also treats `+999` as a default elevation difference that is disallowed in some zone AO certification cases. Both numbers appear in TRRP history; the v3 sentence that defines the public field names 9999.0. — [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf). A blank and a 9999.0 are therefore both “not reported and/or not used,” and they are not the same storage choice. Local extremes such as −9980 or 987 are not that documented code.
- `baseFloodElevation`. Dictionary: “the elevation in feet from the Elevation Certificate at which there is a 1% chance per year of flooding.” The v3 sentence does not define a blank or a sentinel. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). TRRP does: BFE “as shown on effective FIRM in tenths of feet. Value of 9999.0 indicates the field is not reported and/or used for this policy.” Zone AO uses depth rather than an elevation. Change 26 allows PRP, Group Flood, MPPP, Emergency Program, provisional, and tentative policies to “report the default (9999.0) or blank,” and allows 9999.0 on current-map BFE for unnumbered A, AO, AR, A99, B, C, D, unnumbered V, and X. — [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf); [TRRP Change 26](https://nfipservices.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/trrp_change_26.pdf). Zone labels in the v3 `ratedFloodZone` definition match that split: A and V have “no Base Flood Elevation on FIRM”; AE and VE have a BFE on the FIRM; AO is sheet flow. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3).
- `lowestAdjacentGrade`. v3 dictionary defines the grade and does not define a blank. TRRP: “Value 9999.0 indicates the field is not reported and/or used for this policy.” Whether it is required depends on the new/rollover/transfer indicator. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3); [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf).
- `lowestFloorElevation`. v3 dictionary: elevation in feet of the reference level “from the Elevation Certificate.” No blank rule. TRRP: “supported by an elevation survey of the property. Value 9999.0 indicates the field is not reported and/or used for this policy.” TRRP also notes that the claims master file stores whole feet, while the policy file uses tenths. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3); [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf).
- `disasterAssistanceCoverageRequired`. Dictionary: which federal agency required flood insurance as a condition of disaster assistance. Legal values include “0 : Not Required,” plus SBA, FEMA, a canceled HHS code, and other. No sentence defines a blank. Code 0 is an observed value, not an empty cell. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). TRRP reporting requirement: “Required with the exception of MPPP Policies and Group Flood Insurance Policies.” — [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf).
- `obstructionType`. Dictionary: obstruction under an elevated building (enclosure and/or machinery below the lowest elevated floor). Codes run from 10 “Free of obstruction” through enclosure, breakaway, and elevator codes. The v3 table does not say what a blank means. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). TRRP: the element “describes the type of obstruction that may be present under an elevated building.” Code 60, “With obstruction,” “may [be] use[d] when insufficient information exists to determine a more specific obstruction type” for crawlspaces without proper openings on pre-FIRM buildings without elevations. A later note in the same plan: “Obstruction type 60 is a default value for ‘unknown.’” CRS matrices explicitly allow a blank obstruction type on post-FIRM non-elevated buildings. — [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf). `elevatedBuildingIndicator` is the NFIP elevated-building definition (no basement; floor above grade; anchored; pilings, columns, shear walls, or solid perimeter walls). — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3).
- `nonPaymentReasonBuilding` and `nonPaymentReasonContents`. Dictionary title is the reason the claim “was not paid,” and the TRRP name printed in the dictionary is “Claim Closed Without Payment Reason.” Codes 01–20, 97, 98, 99 cover denial below the deductible, seepage, not a flood, failure to pursue, wind, no demonstrable damage, other, and erroneous assignment. No blank rule. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). TRRP: the element “Indicates the reason a building claim was closed without payment” (and the contents analogue “a contents claim case”). “REPORTING REQUIREMENT: Required.” Transaction `46A` is “Close Claim/Loss Without Payment.” Separately, “A closed without payment is counted as a case, but is not counted as a claim.” — [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf). The field’s job is the reason on a without-payment close. It is not a general claim-status code.

**Structural or partial**

- `basementEnclosureCrawlspaceType`. Codes: 0 none, 1 finished basement/enclosure, 2 unfinished, 3 crawlspace, 4 subgrade crawlspace. Blank is not among the legal values. Code 0 already means none. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3).
- `locationOfContents`. Codes 1–7 for basement only through manufactured home. No blank code and no “no contents” code. TRRP: required for policies effective on or after 1996-04-30; for single-family buildings “contents are considered to be located throughout the structure”; policy and claims masters “should be in agreement.” — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3); [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf).
- `contentsDamageAmount`. “The actual cash value amount of damage to contents in whole dollars.” `contentsPropertyValue`: actual cash value of contents “as estimated by an adjuster.” `contentsReplacementCost`: “Estimated cost in whole dollars to replace the contents as reported by the insurer.” None of the three defines a blank. Zero would be a reported amount, not an empty cell. The dictionary also says personal property stored in a basement is not covered, which is a coverage rule, not a missingness rule. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). `totalContentsInsuranceCoverage` is “Total Insurance Amount in whole dollars on the Contents,” so an explicit zero coverage is a filled value meaning no contents limit. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3).
- `interiorWaterDepth` and `exteriorWaterDepth`. v3: exterior is “depth of water, in inches, for a main building based on exterior water marks relative to the lowest adjacent grade.” Interior uses “interior water marks” and, in the v3 wording, is also “relative to the lowest adjacent grade.” — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). TRRP Change 26 disagrees on the interior reference and adds the sentinel. Exterior, main building: water marks “relative to the lowest adjacent grade.” “In the case where only an appurtenant building has been damaged and water has not reached the main building, then the water depth is coded as +999.” Interior, main building: relative to the lowest floor (top of floor in non-V zones; bottom of the floor beam in V zones), not the lowest adjacent grade; basement example uses a negative depth. The same “has not reached the main building” case “is coded as +999.” Edit rule: “positive or negative whole number (reported in inches).” Reporting requirement: required. Source: adjuster’s report. — [TRRP Change 26](https://nfipservices.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/trrp_change_26.pdf). Bulletin W-16022: beginning 2016-10-01, insurers “will be required to report” exterior and interior depth of water. — [W-16022](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/bulletins/W-16022/w-16022.pdf). So for 2020–2025 these fields were already required. A blank is not the +999 code. +999 is “water did not reach the main building,” not “depth unknown.”
- `contentsDeductibleCode`. Legal values map codes to dollar deductibles. Code 0 is “$500,” not “no contents coverage” and not “zero deductible.” Codes continue through H ($200, group policies only). No blank definition. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3).
- `crsClassCode`. Classes 1–10 with stated SFHA and non-SFHA premium credits; class 10 is 0 percent / 0 percent. “Beginning with Risk Rating 2.0, there are no longer percentage discount differences for policies inside and outside of the SFHA.” The dictionary does not say a blank means “community is not in CRS,” and it does not say to store non-participants as class 10. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3).
- `buildingDescriptionCode`. Use of the building, codes 01 main house through 20 townhouse/rowhouse. No blank definition. `occupancyType` is a separate filled code for rating occupancy, including two-digit Risk Rating 2.0 codes. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3).
- `floodEvent`. “Name given to a flooding catastrophe.” No blank definition. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). Read with the FAQ sentence that an EDN is not assigned for every disaster. — [FAQs About NFIP Data](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data). TRRP’s older catastrophe section told companies to identify each loss by FICO “when applicable” and to “report only the FICO designation that is provided to them.” — [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf).
- `floodZoneCurrent`. Current FIRM zone where the property “is currently located,” with the same zone glossary as `ratedFloodZone` (“Formerly called floodZone,” the zone “used to rate the insured property”). No blank definition. The FAQ notes that NFIP rules “allow properties to be rated in one flood zone, while physically located in another,” so the two zone fields are different facts. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3); [FAQs About NFIP Data](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data).
- `eventDesignationNumber`. “A unique identifier established for a catastrophe flood event,” format like `TX0220`, replacing FICO after June 2020. “A public bulletin will designate the EDN number assignments.” — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). Absence of an assignment is contemplated by the FAQ. — [FAQs About NFIP Data](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data).
- `amountPaidOnBuildingClaim` and `amountPaidOnContentsClaim`. Dictionary: dollar amount paid. “A negative amount may appear” when a check was not cashed and had to be reissued. No sentence says null means zero. Net building payment “is the amountPaidOnBuildingClaim minus any recovery amounts” (contents analogue uses the contents paid amount). — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). TRRP loss status can be “paid” or “pending.” Pending includes a case reserve with no payment, and claims that are “part paid and part on reserve.” — [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf). The public FAQ says “the current claims data doesn’t include payment date or claims transaction” and that disbursements cannot be reported by calendar year from date of loss. — [FAQs About NFIP Data](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data). That FAQ sentence conflicts with the v3 field `mostRecentPaymentDate` (“The most recent date a claim payment was issued to insured”). The dataset page is the later field list. Neither source says a null paid-amount is a zero.
- `waterDepth`. v3: “Depth of flood water in inches. Note: there are instances where measurements were provided in feet.” No sentinel in the v3 sentence. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). TRRP “Water Depth - Relative to Main Building” is a length-3 signed integer. The narrative examples are in feet (“−2 feet,” “+2 feet”). “In the case where only an appurtenant building has been damaged and water has not reached the main building, then the water depth is coded as +99.” Required, from the adjuster’s report. — [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf). So 99 is a documented code for “water has not reached the main building,” not a generic 99XX unknown. The inch-versus-foot conflict is also documented: v3 says inches and warns that some measurements were in feet; the TRRP examples for this older field are in feet, while Change 26’s newer exterior and interior fields are explicitly inches.
- `buildingDamageAmount`. “The actual cash value amount of damage to a main property in whole dollars.” No blank rule. A reported zero is a dollar amount. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). Related cause-of-damage codes, which are filled values, include B “Expedited claim handling process without site inspection,” C follow-up site inspection, and D remote adjustment. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3).
- `latitude` and `longitude`. v3: approximate location “to 1 decimal place.” “The precision has been lessened to ensure individual privacy. This may result in a point location that exists in an incorrect county or state. Use the state and county fields for record aggregation.” — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). The FAQ repeats truncation to one decimal, about six miles, and says census tract and county “are derived from a policy or claim geocode” and are the preferred aggregation fields. “More recent data will be more reliable for geographical analysis than older data.” — [FAQs About NFIP Data](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data). Truncation is applied to coordinates that exist. The dictionary does not define the remaining blank coordinates.
- `originalConstructionDate`. v3: “The original date of the construction of the building.” No placeholder rule. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). TRRP: building-permit date, construction date, or substantial-improvement date. “If the construction date is a date prior to the year 1900, report this date as January 1, 1900.” That is the only January 1 placeholder the plan states. — [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf).
- `reportedCity`. “This is the city of the insured property as reported to us by our Write Your Own (WYO) partners. NOTE: the city value is currently unavailable.” — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3). Combined with the FAQ withholding of street address as PII, the constant string is a publication suppression, not a city name that happens to be missing on some rows.

ICC context the structural question needs: `amountPaidOnIncreasedCostOfComplianceClaim` “provides up to $30,000” for mitigation after a flood. `iccCoverage` is the coverage limit. The FAQ says FEMA does not determine substantial damage; the program receives that information from communities “when a claim receives payment following a Substantial Damage determination,” which can include ICC. — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3); [FAQs About NFIP Data](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data). Risk Rating 2.0 excludes “ICC claims, closed without payments claims, and loss avoidance measures” from the prior-claims surcharge count. — [Risk Rating 2.0 FAQ](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/fema-nfip-risk-rating-2.0-FAQs.pdf).

### Inferences

- For this extract, empty `ficoNumber` is the retired identifier, not a redaction and not an unknown event id. `eventDesignationNumber` is the replacement, and only for events FEMA designated. January–May 2020 is inside the FICO era on the dictionary’s own cutoff, so a 100 percent null that includes those months is slightly stronger than the June 2020 sentence. The column was not omitted; it is present and empty.
- Empty `floodCharacteristicsIndicator` and almost-empty `floodWaterDuration` match optional adjuster items. They are not documented as redacted, not documented as 99XX sentinels, and not documented as “no flood.” A 100 percent empty optional column is also consistent with the FAQ practice of losing confidence in a field, but FEMA did not name this column as one it omitted. The column is still on the v3 schema.
- Recovery date and recovery dollars are written when a recovery transaction exists. Their emptiness is the ordinary state of a claim with no salvage, subrogation, or overpayment recovery. That is a meaningful absence. It is not a definition, stated by FEMA, that the dollar amount is the number zero. Salvage that was netted out of the original payment is, by TRRP, not in the salvage field at all.
- Empty `elevationCertificateIndicator` means “not reported at the time the policy was written.” It is item nonresponse by FEMA’s definition. It is not “no certificate.” Risk Rating 2.0 makes non-reporting expected on newer business, because the certificate is optional for purchase and for rating.
- Elevation numbers (`elevationDifference`, and by TRRP also BFE, lowest adjacent grade, and lowest floor elevation) use 9999.0 for “not reported and/or not used for this policy.” A true null in the public file is a second way to store that same idea, or a value dropped in publication. Zones without a BFE on the FIRM, and rating methods TRRP allows to report the default, make many of these blanks structural. Code 9999.0 should be recoded to a non-numeric “not used / not reported” level before any numeric summary. Other huge values are not that code; OIG’s “invalid entries” migrated into PIVOT are the relevant data-quality background, not a second official sentinel.
- `disasterAssistanceCoverageRequired` already has a real zero: code 0, “Not Required.” A blank is not that code. TRRP still called the element required except for MPPP and group policies. The dictionary does not explain a majority-blank column.
- `obstructionType` is defined for elevated buildings. Blank on a non-elevated building is the state TRRP’s CRS rules already allow. ## How should Little and Rubin be applied so a student notebook can label MCAR vs MAR vs MNAR without overclaiming?

### Takeaway

Use the Rubin definitions only for item nonresponse, state them as assumptions, and let the notebook’s strongest claim be “MCAR is implausible given this observed association” or “MAR is a working assumption conditional on these observed fields.” MAR versus MNAR cannot be settled from the observed table. Structural blanks, sentinels, and privacy suppression do not get a mechanism label.

### Cited Findings

- The standard classification is Rubin’s. Missingness is MCAR when the probability of a missing value does not depend on the observed or the unobserved data, MAR when it depends only on observed data, and MNAR when it depends on the unobserved values themselves. MCAR and MAR are the “ignorable” mechanisms for likelihood inference when the missingness parameters are distinct from the data parameters; MNAR is not ignorable. — Fielding, Maclennan, Cook, and Ramsay, summarizing Rubin (1976) and Little and Rubin, [Investigating the missing data mechanism](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2711047/)
- Little’s 1988 test checks a consequence of MCAR: groups defined by the missingness pattern should have the same means on the variables they all observe. A significant result rejects MCAR. It does not choose between MAR and MNAR. A non-significant result does not prove MCAR. — same review, describing Little’s test, [PMC2711047](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2711047/)
- A practical restatement used in teaching: MCAR is independent of every variable; MAR may depend on observed variables and, given those, not on the missing value; MNAR depends on the missing value even after the observed variables are accounted for. Little’s test “rules out the strongest assumption, but does not tell you whether the true mechanism is MAR or MNAR.” Failure to reject “does not prove MCAR.” — [MCAR, MAR and MNAR: Diagnosing Your Missing Data](https://casrai.org/guides/mcar-mar-and-mnar-diagnosing-your-missing-data)
- Published NFIP claims analyses have handled incomplete rows by dropping them, which is valid only under MCAR and was not given a mechanism test in the text retrieved here. Michel-Kerjan and Kousky, using single-family Florida claims for 2000–2006, wrote that “data were missing for one or more variables for some of the observations, reducing the sample size,” then analyzed the cleaned remainder (more than 42,000 claims) and cautioned about omitted variables. — [Come Rain or Shine, Journal of Risk and Insurance 2010](https://repec.udesa.edu.ar/pub/Finanzas/Journals/Journal%20of%20Risk%20&%20Insurance/2010/Vol.%2077%20Issue%202/50211367.pdf)
- Kousky and Michel-Kerjan’s later national study analyzed “over 1 million claims” from 1978–2012. The abstract retrieved here does not classify missingness as MCAR, MAR, or MNAR. — [Examining Flood Insurance Claims in the United States, doi:10.1111/jori.12106](https://doi.org/10.1111/jori.12106)
- OIG documented missing data and invalid values in the system that feeds this file, including errors carried into PIVOT in 2019. That is evidence of a data-quality process, not a Rubin test. — [OIG-21-04](https://www.oig.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/assets/2020-11/OIG-21-04-Nov20.pdf)

### Inferences

Labels the notebook can write, and the sentence that must sit next to each label:

- Do not label. `reportedCity`. `ficoNumber` for losses after June 2020. Sentinel 99 and 999 on water depth. Sentinel 9999.0 on elevation fields. Obstruction blank when the building is not elevated. Non-payment reason blank when that coverage was paid. Recovery fields blank when no recovery was recorded. Event name and EDN blank when the claim is the undesignated remainder. Contents measures blank when contents coverage is 0. One-decimal coordinates that are present. January 1 dates that are present. These are design, suppression, sentinels, or inapplicability. Rubin’s R indicator is for a value that exists and was not seen.
- MCAR is not a reasonable working assumption for any high-missing column in this list. The reporting rules already name observed covariates: elevated building, flood zone, rate method, contents coverage, paid versus without-payment close, cause-of-damage inspection codes, and whether a catastrophe number was issued. If missingness tracks any of those, Little’s test should reject MCAR. The notebook can say “MCAR rejected” only after that test, or after a simple grouped comparison the student shows. It can say “MCAR is implausible on substantive grounds” before the test, and should not say “MCAR” as the finding.
- MAR as a working assumption, always with the conditioning variables named, and always with the next bullet’s caveat:
  - Obstruction nulls given `elevatedBuildingIndicator`. If the indicator fully accounts for the blanks, missingness is a function of an observed covariate. That is the MAR pattern, and it is also the structural pattern. The statistical label does not make the obstruction type exist.
  - Elevation-certificate and elevation-number nulls given `rateMethod`, `ratedFloodZone`, `postFIRMConstructionIndicator`, and whether the policy term falls under Risk Rating 2.0. The certificate’s empty-means-not-reported definition plus the 2021–2023 rule change are observed reasons to skip the field.
  - Depth and building-damage nulls given `causeOfDamage` in {B, C, D} and given basement or elevated status. Those cause codes are observed inspection regimes.
  - Contents-field nulls given `totalContentsInsuranceCoverage` and occupancy. After removing the zero-coverage rows, any leftover association with occupancy or WYO-side reporting is still only a reason to prefer MAR over MCAR.
  - Current-zone and coordinate nulls given whether county and ZIP are present. The FAQ says county and tract are derived from the geocode, so the notebook should check that co-missingness rather than assume geography is fully observed.
  - Disaster-assistance blanks given policy form, if MPPP and group-flood rows can be identified from `rateMethod` (TRRP names those rating methods as the exception).
- MNAR is not rejected for the item-nonresponse remainder, and the notebook should say why in one substantive sentence rather than “it might be MNAR”:
  - Elevation and certificate: a building’s true elevation can influence whether anyone orders a certificate, because Risk Rating 2.0 still lets the owner submit one “for a potentially more favorable premium.” Conditioning on zone and rate method does not observe that choice.
  - Optional flood duration and flood characteristics: an adjuster who records hours or velocity is more likely to do so when the water was unusual. With duration empty on 94.4 percent of rows and characteristics empty on all of them, the filled duration values are a selected subset. There is no within-file test of characteristics at all.
  - Damage and depth when the site was not inspected, or when water marks were absent because damage was total or because water never entered: the missingness can depend on the unobserved depth. Cause code B explains some of this and leaves the rest open.
  - Ungeocoded coordinates: bad or nonstandard addresses can be missing because of the address itself. A 0.66 percent rate does not make this MCAR. Little’s test has little to say about a rare pattern.
  - January 1 day-of-month, if the student treats the day as the variable: the day is set to 1 because the true day was not known. That is dependence on the unobserved day. The year can still be analyzed as coarsened, with a flag. Only 1900-01-01 is the documented rule.
- Shared patterns are one mechanism, not many. One indicator for “elevation block unreported,” one for “contents block unreported,” one for “recovery recorded,” one for “designated event.” Testing each column’s MCAR status separately will double-count the same switch.
- “Ignorable” is not a synonym for “safe to fill” and is not a synonym for “safe to drop.” The 2010 Florida claims paper dropped incomplete rows and said the sample got smaller. The notebook should not copy that step under a silent MCAR assumption. Listwise deletion is the procedure that needs MCAR. This note does not recommend a replacement procedure.
- Wording template the notebook can use without overclaiming: “After removing structural blanks and sentinels, missingness in [field] is associated with [observed fields], so MCAR is rejected. A MAR working assumption conditional on those fields is reasonable for description. MNAR remains possible because [one substantive reason]. The observed data cannot separate those two.”

What the evidence does not support: a single mechanism for a whole column that mixes structural blanks and nonresponse; a claim that empty elevation-certificate means the property has no certificate; a claim that class-10 CRS, code-0 basement, code-0 contents deductible, or code-0 disaster assistance are the filled versions of the blanks beside them.

### Gaps

- No Little’s test, logistic missingness model, or pattern table was computed for this note. The MAR working assumptions above are substantive, from the reporting rules, not confirmed associations in the 2020–2025 extract.
- The full text of Kousky and Michel-Kerjan (Journal of Risk and Insurance, 2017) was not available in this pass, so their treatment of incomplete NFIP claims fields is not reported here. The 2010 Florida paper is the one that states they dropped incomplete observations.
- Little and Rubin’s textbook was not opened page by page. The mechanism definitions rest on Rubin’s framework as summarized by the Fielding et al. review and the secondary diagnostic guide. If a course requires a page citation, the student still needs the book.
- Distinctness of parameters, the other half of Rubin’s “ignorability,” is not something this file can show. The notebook should not use the word “ignorable” for these columns.

## Which missingness patterns are informative and must be kept as their own signal rather than treated as holes to fill?

### Takeaway

Several of the largest “holes” are the measurement. Collapsing them to a filled number, or dropping the rows, deletes the fact FEMA or the adjuster actually recorded: no designated event, no payment, water did not reach the building, elevation not used, certificate not reported, no recovery, city withheld, FICO retired.

### Cited Findings

- Obstruction type 60 is the default when “insufficient information exists.” — [TRRP Plan](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2025-07/jan_2015_consolidated_trrp.pdf)
- An EDN is not assigned for every disaster; undesignated claims are queried by state and date of loss. — [FAQs About NFIP Data](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/flood-maps-and-data/faqs-about-nfip-data)
- Non-payment reason codes are the reason a coverage was closed without payment, including “less than deductible,” “not actual flood,” and “no demonstrable damage.” — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)

- Cause-of-damage codes B, C, and D record expedited or remote adjustment, including “without site inspection.” — [v3 dictionary](https://www.fema.gov/openfema-data-page/nfip-redacted-claims-v3)
- Local extract, not a FEMA statistic: net payment is 0 wherever the gross building or contents paid amount is null (7.85 percent). Contents damage, value, and replacement cost are empty together (37.8 percent). About 55 percent of construction dates are January 1. Recovery amounts and the recovery date are empty together (99.85 percent).

### Inferences

Keep these as variables in their own right. Do not treat them as holes to be filled, and do not drop them as if they were noise.

- `event_designated`: 0 when `floodEvent` is blank, with a second flag if `eventDesignationNumber` is blank while the name is present, or the reverse. The 13-point rate gap is itself a data fact. The name, when present, is a group key, not a numeric feature to complete.
- `closed_without_payment_building` and `closed_without_payment_contents`: 1 when the reason code is present. The code is the reason. A blank reason on a paid coverage is the complement, not a missing reason. A blank reason on a zero net payment is a data-quality flag, separate from the reason.
- `no_payment_recorded`: 1 when the paid amount is null and the net amount is 0 in this extract. Store the dollars as 0 only beside that flag. A negative paid amount stays negative; the dictionary says those are reissued checks, not missing data.
- `water_did_not_reach_main`: 1 when `waterDepth` is 99 or exterior/interior depth is 999. Keep a separate `depth_unreported` for true nulls. Converting 99 or 999 to null erases the only documented meaning of those numbers. Negative depths stay negative; TRRP’s basement and V-zone examples are real measurements below the reference level.
- `elevation_not_used_or_unreported`: 1 when elevation difference, BFE, lowest adjacent grade, or lowest floor elevation is 9999.0 or blank for a structural reason. `elevation_certificate_not_reported`: 1 when the indicator is empty. That second flag means “not reported,” not “no certificate.”
- `obstruction_unknown`: 1 when the code is 60. `obstruction_not_applicable`: 1 when the building is not elevated. Do not put either row in an “obstruction type missing” bin that then gets a modal enclosure code.
- `recovery_recorded`: 1 when any of the recovery amounts or `mostRecentRecoveryDate` is filled. The 99.85 percent empty mass is the base rate of a rare event. The dollar value matters only inside the recorded slice. Because TRRP excludes salvage that was netted from the original check, this flag is “a later recovery was booked,” not “the insured received no salvage credit.”
- `contents_measures_unreported`: 1 when contents coverage is positive and the contents money fields are empty. When coverage is 0, the signal is `no_contents_coverage`, and the empty money fields add nothing.
- `damage_unreported`: 1 when `buildingDamageAmount` is null. A recorded 0 stays 0. The same split for contents damage after the coverage check.
- `construction_date_day_unknown`: 1 when the date is January 1 and the year is not the documented 1900 substitute, or including 1900-01-01 if the student wants every official placeholder flagged. The year remains usable. The flag is the signal that the day was not collected.
- `geocode_missing`: 1 on the 0.66 percent with no latitude or longitude. Do not flag the one-decimal coordinates as missing. Do flag `reportedCity` as withheld for the whole table so it is not read as a place name.
- `adjuster_report_thin`: optional, descriptive only. A row with empty duration, empty flood characteristics, empty interior depth, and a B/C/D cause code is a thin inspection record. The cause code is the documented part. The rest is a pattern, not a FEMA index.

Patterns that look like missingness and are not signals about the claim:

- `ficoNumber` all null in this window. The signal lives on the EDN and the event name. Keeping a “FICO missing” indicator would be a constant.
- `reportedCity` all “Currently Unavailable.” A missingness indicator would be a constant. The information is “FEMA did not publish city.”
- One-decimal coordinates. The privacy coarsening is a property of every published point. A missingness indicator on those points would mark the points that are present.

### Gaps

- No source gives the joint distribution of these flags in the 2020–2025 extract. In particular, the overlap of `floodEvent` blank with `eventDesignationNumber` blank, of contents-money blanks with zero contents coverage, and of elevation 9999.0 with zone and rate method, still has to be counted in the notebook before the flags above are treated as clean.
- FEMA does not say that a null gross payment is always a closed-without-payment claim. The “no payment recorded” flag is justified by the local net-payment finding, not by the dictionary.
- Whether interior depth is referenced to the lowest floor (TRRP) or the lowest adjacent grade (v3 dictionary) changes the meaning of a negative interior depth. The flag for 999 does not resolve that unit and reference-level conflict.
